Song or neighbours?

Anna-Ilona Nikolaeva, Yauheni Zviazdou and Tim Kicker
Introduction to Data Science, University of Helsinki, October 2026

Since 2016 Cyprus has made it to the Eurovision final eight times. Every single time, the Greek public gave it twelve points1. Was that the song or was that Greece?

We went through every vote in the ten finals from 2016 to 2026 and kept the jury and the public apart. For each vote we asked how much of it the song explains and how much the relationship between the two countries.

For the song we look at how well it did with everybody else. For the two countries we look at how close they are (distance, a shared border or language) and how they voted for each other in earlier years. The shares say how much of the differences in points each part predicts, always on finals the model never saw. Depending on the model the two countries count 4.2 to 4.5 times as much for the public.

A high number does not mean that anyone cheated. Neighbours often share a music market or a language. Many people living in one country also come from the other and still vote for home.

Who this is for

Fans can see how often a country rewards its neighbours and look up their own country. Delegations can see which part of a result came from the song and which part came from neighbours and diaspora.

How we did it

We put the results of every final since 1957 next to what connects each pair of countries (distance, a shared border or language). A model learned from nine finals at a time and was always tested on the tenth. The technical report has the details.

For every pair of countries we compare the points one gave the other with what the song got from everybody else. The difference, averaged over all finals the two countries shared, shows who favours whom. Only pairs that met in at least three finals are listed.
